The richness of Dostoyevsky's White Nights lies in the delusional passion of its narrator’s expressive monologues. So, it's interesting that Robert Bresson, a director known for his aversion to performed emotion, decided to adapt a novella that lives in what he'd call "movement from the interior to the exterior." 

Instead of adjusting his restrained style, Bresson elects to draw emotional flavor from the melancholy colors and sounds of the Parisian streets. As Jacques holds Marthe, the camera lingers on a bossa nova band on a bateau mouche floating through the Seine, reflecting the haunting romanticism the narrator does not express. A seeming mismatch between the text and filmmaker produces a hypnotic translation of Dostoyevsky’s dialogue-heavy work on to film.
