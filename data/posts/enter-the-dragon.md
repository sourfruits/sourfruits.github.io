*Originally written for Letterboxd*

What makes this film special is its depiction of a Hong Kong now lost to time. My mom, who grew up there in the 70s, would regale me with tales of the magic of Hong Kong under British rule. To her, it was an era of shared respect and cultural integration. 

In the movie, British intelligence and Shaolin masters work with mutual respect. The scenes in the harbor show the visual dichotomy of a modernizing Hong Kong. Sampan rowers in baggy work jackets ferry passengers to and from larger junk vessels. In the background, Western-style skyscrapers loom, indicative of the skyline to come. Bruce Lee’s character straddles cultures through fashion, alternating between slick tailored Western suits and traditional kung fu garb. 

70s HK is a time I will never fully experience, only catch glimpses of — this film is pulpy exploitation that gives life to the world of my mother’s stories.
