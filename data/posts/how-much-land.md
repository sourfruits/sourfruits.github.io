The short stories of Leo Tolstoy, who is often cited as one of the greatest writers ever, are surprisingly simple. Penguin Classic’s *How Much Land Does a Man Need & Other Stories* collects such stories from across his career — from early military tales in the Caucasus, to late-career moral parables of a biblical nature. On the surface, each short story is minimal. The prose is light and natural, characters are not over-developed, and parables have clear, obvious morals. Yet, Tolstoy’s stylistic simplicity belies an underlying profundity. The contexts of war and allegory strip the stories bare, accentuating enduring truths about human nature under duress, integrity, and the beauty of God’s creation.
